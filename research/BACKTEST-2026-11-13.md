# Backtest of long-term portfolios, 1928 to 2025

Run 2026-11-13 with `scripts/backtest.py`. This is a fair backtest because it replays fixed rules on public data. It does not replay the Scout's own picks, which would only measure memory.

**Data:** Damodaran (NYU) yearly returns for bonds, gold, house prices and inflation; Ken French (Dartmouth) monthly returns for the whole US stock market, small cheap companies, and recent winners. 98 years.
**Method:** the software tried all 92,378 mixes of ten building blocks in 10% steps. Each mix was scored by what a saver adding money every year would have ended with over every 20-year stretch in history.
All returns are after inflation and before taxes.

## The building blocks alone
| Block | Return per year | Worst year | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whole US stock market | 6.8% | -38% | the plain choice |
| Small cheap companies | 10.1% | -52% | after 0.5% yearly cost |
| Recent winners (momentum) | 11.2% | -48% | after 1.5% yearly cost |
| Stocks with a trend rule | 6.3% | -22% | in stocks only while above the 10-month average, else cash |
| Small cheap companies with the trend rule | 9.6% | -43% | |
| Stocks at 2x with the trend rule | 9.8% | -45% | borrowing cost 1.5% a year above cash |
| 10-year Treasury bonds | 1.5% | -23% | |
| Gold | 2.5% | -38% | |
| Houses, with modeled rent | 5.0% | -13% | rent of 4% a year after costs is an assumption |
| Cash | 0.2% | -15% | |

## What the software chose
| Goal | Mix | Return per year | Worst fall on the monthly path |
|---|---|---|---|
| Highest typical result | 90% small cheap, 10% momentum | 10.4% | -87% |
| **Best result when luck is bad** | **30% small cheap, 20% momentum, 40% stocks at 2x with the trend rule, 10% gold** | **10.7%** | **-69%** |
| Same goal, no borrowing | 20% small cheap, 40% momentum, 30% small cheap with the trend rule, 10% gold | 10.6% | -58% |
| Plain stocks, for comparison | 100% stock market | 6.8% | -84% |

The bold mix beat or matched plain stocks in 8 of 9 decades (it trailed slightly in the 2010s) and returned 9.3% a year from 2000 to 2025 against 5.5% for plain stocks.

## Walk-forward check (the honest part)
- Mixes chosen using only 1928 to 1976, then run on 1977 to 2025: 11.0 to 11.2% a year, against 8.1% for plain stocks.
- Mixes chosen using only 1977 to 2025, then run on 1928 to 1976: 9.9 to 10.6% a year, against 5.5%.
The exact mix changed between halves, but the direction held both ways: tilt toward small cheap companies and recent winners, and use a trend rule.

## Side checks
- **Rental house with 25% down (modeled, 1972 to 2025):** 7.5% a year against 6.9% for stocks, with a -50% worst year, and it takes work. Not better than the mix on these numbers.
- **Bitcoin (2015 to 2025 only):** a 5% slice would have lifted the mix from 12.9% to 19.7% a year before inflation. Eleven years of one asset that started near zero proves nothing about the next twenty. If used at all, keep it small.
- **Trend signal today:** on (stocks 5.8% above their 10-month average at the end of September 2026). The rule switches about 1.5 times a year.

## Why the real result will be lower than the table
1. **These edges are now well known.** Small cheap companies returned 7.8% a year from 2010 to 2025 against 11.1% for the market. The "2 points worse" rows are the more realistic guide.
2. **Taxes are not counted.** The trend rule and momentum create taxable gains. They belong in a retirement account.
3. **Costs are estimates.** Real funds differ from these research portfolios.
4. **The 2x block has a risk monthly data cannot show.** A one-day crash like October 1987 would hit it at double size before the rule could react.
5. **Everyone who held these mixes sat through falls of more than half.** The result only exists for a person who did not sell.

## Follow-up
The 2x block was later rebuilt from daily data and the whole result was put through eleven stress tests: see `ROBUSTNESS-2026-11-13.md`. The mix and the figures above held.

## What this changes in the Scout
The core is no longer "the plain index." It is the software mix, checked monthly with one mechanical signal. The backtest is rerun every January on the new year of data, and the Scout reports if the winning mix changes.
