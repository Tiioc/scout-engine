## 6. Checking more often

Daily data, 1926 to 2026. Same growth mix, exits checked at different speeds.

| Exit check | Growth per year | Deepest fall | Worst day | Worst week | Trades per year |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| monthly check (the current rule) | 11.9% | -60% | -16% | -31% | 4.5 |
| weekly check | 12.3% | -47% | -15% | -31% | 8.6 |
| weekly check, 2% buffer | 12.0% | -46% | -16% | -31% | 4.4 |
| weekly check, 4% buffer | 11.9% | -47% | -16% | -31% | 3.0 |
| daily check | 12.3% | -56% | -14% | -30% | 17.4 |
| daily check, 2% buffer | 11.9% | -54% | -14% | -30% | 5.7 |
| daily check, 4% buffer | 12.1% | -46% | -14% | -30% | 3.5 |

- **Weekly beats monthly.** With a 2% buffer it has the same growth and the same number of trades, and the deepest fall drops from 60% to 46%. This held in both halves of history (46% against 60%, and 32% against 37%).
- **Daily is no better than weekly.** It trades more or falls further.
- **Daily data showed the monthly rule's real worst fall was 60%, not the 50% seen in month-end data.** The earlier figure understated it.
- **Nothing avoids a sudden crash.** The worst day (about -17%) and worst week (about -31%) are the same under every rule.

## What changed in the Scout

1. Exits are now checked weekly against a 200-day average with a 2% buffer.
2. The scheduled check runs weekly.
3. The 2x fund's extra cost and the tax cost are recorded as known drags.
4. No new assets were added.
